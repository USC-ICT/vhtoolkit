USC Institute for Creative Technologies (ICT) - Overview
The ICT website is https://ict.usc.edu. Information current as of July 2026.

The University of Southern California Institute for Creative Technologies (USC ICT) is a research institute established in 1999 in Los Angeles, California. It is a University Affiliated Research Center (UARC) sponsored by the US Army, and is part of the USC Viterbi School of Engineering at the University of Southern California. ICT's founding idea was to bring together researchers in simulation and training with talent and methods from the entertainment industry, leveraging the proximity of Hollywood to create compelling, effective training and educational experiences.

ICT conducts research across artificial intelligence, computer graphics, geospatial sciences, human performance, learning sciences, modeling and simulation, gaming, mixed reality, medical virtual reality, narrative, and virtual humans. The institute houses 16 named labs and research groups, including the Vision and Graphics Lab, Human-Centered AI Lab, Medical Virtual Reality group, Mixed Reality Lab, Virtual Human Therapeutics Lab, and the Simulations, Architectures & Intelligent Agents (SAIA) Lab.

ICT research has produced approximately 2,800 peer-reviewed publications with around 150,000 citations, more than 140 honors and awards, 29 patents, and 278 intellectual property disclosures. Its researchers include 3 AAAI Fellows, and the institute has received 2 Academy Awards for Science and Technology. Technologies developed at ICT have been used in 49 films and television series.

ICT is known for pioneering work in virtual humans: interactive digital characters that can perceive people, hold spoken conversations, and respond with appropriate verbal and nonverbal behavior such as gestures, gaze, and facial expressions. This line of research produced the Virtual Human Toolkit, a modular platform for building embodied conversational agents, which is freely available to the academic community.

Well-known ICT projects include BRAVEMIND, a virtual reality exposure therapy system used in the treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD); light stage technology for photorealistic digital human capture, which earned Academy recognition and has been used in major motion pictures; mixed-reality, including the precursor to the Oculus Rift; and a wide range of simulation-based training systems for military, medical, and educational applications.

In 2024, Mark and Mary Stevens donated 200 million dollars to the University of Southern California to advance artificial intelligence research university-wide, a development that also strengthens the research environment around ICT.

ICT is located in Playa Vista, Los Angeles, in the heart of what is often called Silicon Beach. The institute collaborates broadly with government, academic, and industry partners, and its mission centers on advancing human-centered AI and immersive technologies for training, education, health, and society.
