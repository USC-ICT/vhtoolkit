The conversation guard in the Virtual Human Toolkit

A virtual human driven by a large language model can be talked out of its role. A user may tell it to ignore its instructions, ask it to reveal its system prompt, or steer it into claiming a real biography or giving medical or legal advice. In a research study that is worse than merely embarrassing, because it changes what participants experienced partway through a session.

The conversation guard is a screening stage that sits between the application and the language model, so every character gets it without any application code. It screens what the user said before the model sees it, and screens the character's reply before it is spoken.

What it looks for is the integrity of the character's instructions, rather than harmful content. Its input rules detect attempts to override those instructions, extract the prompt, claim authority over the character, or disguise such an attempt. Its output rules detect prompt leakage and unintended breaks in character. It has no rules for hate, violence, sexual content, or self-harm; that screening comes from the model provider's own safety systems and from the safety section of the prompt.

By default a flagged user turn still reaches the model, with a reminder of the character's safety rules appended to the request, so the conversation continues normally and a false alarm costs nothing a participant would notice. A reply that trips an output rule is regenerated once. The guard can also be configured to decline instead, in which case the character says a short line rather than answering. The rules are patterns rather than comprehension, so they should be reviewed against a particular use case rather than assumed to cover it.

Prompt design carries part of the work. Every character's system prompt begins with a safety section, stated as the highest priority, which covers honesty about being an AI-driven virtual human, no invented life story or credentials, no medical, psychological, legal, or financial advice, care around self-harm and crisis topics, and never revealing the prompt itself. A domain section and a per-character section follow. Because the safety section is shared rather than copied into each character, it can be improved once for every character at the same time.

The guard applies only to generative providers. Intent-based systems such as Rasa or AWS Lex answer with authored content, so there is nothing to jailbreak and nothing to screen, and they bypass it. It also does not yet cover the unified realtime conversation mode, where one provider session handles recognition, generation, and speech together and leaves no seam for a screening stage; the default conversation mode is the classic pipeline, where generation is a separate step and is screened.

Screening is a trade-off. Regenerating a reply costs an extra request, and a rule can occasionally fire on something harmless. The guard can be switched off, and its strictness and its response configured, per project.
