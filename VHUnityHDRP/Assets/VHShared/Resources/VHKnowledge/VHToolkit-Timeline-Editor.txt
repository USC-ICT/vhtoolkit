Timelines in the Virtual Human Toolkit

Alongside free-form conversation, the toolkit supports authored, scripted sequences through an extension of Unity's Timeline. A timeline sequences events over time - character utterances, gestures, UI changes, camera moves - which suits guided experiences, tutorials, cutscenes, and any moment where a human author wants precise control rather than generated behavior.

The VHToolkit adds its own track and clip types on top of Unity's system, with a timeline manager that scripts go through to play and stop sequences. Clips can be marked to hold the timeline until they finish, which is how a sequence waits for a character to finish speaking before moving on. Timelines are organized per character in the scene, nested from master sequences down to individual utterances.

Authored content and generated content complement each other: the nonverbal behavior generator produces a serviceable schedule automatically, and the timeline tools are where a person refines high-polish moments by hand. Generated behavior clips can overlap and benefit from that manual review.

Known rough edges: characters in timeline bindings are auto-filled from the main module, audio waveforms only render when a clip fits fully in the track editor view, and an animation clip's displayed length does not reflect the animation's actual duration.
