Virtual Human Therapeutics Lab (VHTL) at USC ICT - Overview
The VHTL page on the ICT website is https://ict.usc.edu/research/labs-groups/virtual-human-therapeutics-lab/. Information current as of July 2026.

The Virtual Human Therapeutics Lab (VHTL) is a research lab at the University of Southern California Institute for Creative Technologies (USC ICT). The lab is directed by Sharon Mozgai, whose background combines health psychology with virtual reality and virtual human research.

VHTL uses embodied conversational AI agents inside evidence-based mobile health applications to advance mental, emotional, and physical health and to support behavior change. The lab emphasizes equitable and accountable AI practices, pursued through interdisciplinary research, collaboration, and development. Its multidisciplinary team spans researchers, content designers, 3D animators, and interaction designers, with expertise in extended reality and affective computing.

Major projects include Battle Buddy, a mobile wellness application for military service members and veterans, developed with veteran advisors to keep its content authentic; VITA, which helps job-seeking candidates build interview skills by practicing with virtual humans; and Personalized Soldier Avatars, which creates customized, high-fidelity avatars for military training. 

VHTL is a close collaborator of the Simulations, Architectures & Intelligent Agents (SAIA) Lab, which develops the Virtual Human Toolkit and the RIDE platform: VHTL brings the clinical and health-psychology perspective and evidence-based application design, while SAIA provides the virtual human and simulation technology those applications are built on. Projects such as Battle Buddy, VITA, and Personalized Avatars are shared efforts between the two labs, often in collaboration with the MedVR Lab, lead by Skip Rizzo.
