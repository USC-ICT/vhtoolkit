VHToolkit Key Publications

The two main papers to cite when publishing work that uses the Virtual Human Toolkit are the original 2013 introduction paper and the 2022 re-architecture paper. Both were published at IVA, the Intelligent Virtual Agents venue, which is the leading international meeting for research on embodied conversational agents. A third paper, demonstrating the current VHToolkit, appears at IVA 2026.

The 2013 paper is titled "All Together Now: Introducing the Virtual Human Toolkit." Its authors are Arno Hartholt, David Traum, Stacy C. Marsella, Ari Shapiro, Giota Stratou, Anton Leuski, Louis-Philippe Morency, and Jonathan Gratch. It was published at the International Workshop on Intelligent Virtual Agents (IVA 2013), pages 368-381, by Springer. This paper introduced the original Virtual Human Toolkit to the research community.

The 2022 paper is titled "Re-architecting the Virtual Human Toolkit: Towards an Interoperable Platform for Embodied Conversational Agent Research and Development." Its authors are Arno Hartholt, Ed Fast, Zongjian Li, Kevin Kim, Andrew Leeds, and Sharon Mozgai. It was published in the Proceedings of the 22nd ACM International Conference on Intelligent Virtual Agents (IVA 2022), pages 1-8. This paper describes the modern, re-architected VHToolkit built on the RIDE platform, with its interoperable, vendor-agnostic design.

The most recent paper is from 2026, titled "Demonstrating the Open Virtual Human Toolkit: Any Character, Any Technology, Anywhere," by Arno Hartholt, Ed Fast, Andrew Leeds, and Sharon Mozgai. It appears at the ACM International Conference on Intelligent Virtual Agents (IVA 2026) in Puebla, Mexico. It presents the updated toolkit - source code, cloud and local AI services, and multi-platform support, so researchers can create any character, drive it by any technology, and deploy it to any hardware - and reports a preliminary latency analysis: the classic sequential pipeline of speech recognition, language, and synthesis reaches a median of roughly three and a half seconds from the end of user speech to character audio, while the unified continuous-streaming pipeline more than halves that to roughly 1.4 seconds.

Arno Hartholt, who leads the SAIA lab at USC ICT, is the first author on all three papers. Beyond these two citation papers, the VHToolkit and its underlying technologies are described in many other publications by ICT researchers on topics including nonverbal behavior generation, dialogue management, speech processing, and virtual human applications in healthcare and training.
