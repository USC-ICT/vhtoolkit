ICT Labs and Research Groups - Overview

The ICT lab pages are at https://ict.usc.edu/research/labs-groups/. Information, including lab leads, is current as of July 2026.

The USC Institute for Creative Technologies (ICT) organizes its research into the following labs and groups.

Body Computing and Human Performance, led by Leslie Saxon, is a digital health research center that aims to enhance health, wellness, and human performance for service members and society through data analytics and clinical research. It brings together human performance specialists, digital health experts, sensor developers, and designers, with Department of Defense funded research spanning multiple military bases using sensors, software, and data to help prevent injuries in real time.

The Intelligent Human Perception Lab, led by Mohammad Soleymani, focuses on understanding and tracking human behaviors, social signals, and emotions using multimodal machine learning. Research areas include socially assistive technologies for mental health, facial expression analysis, and nonverbal behavior generation. The lab develops open-source tools including OpenSense for multimodal behavior assessment and LibreFace for facial expression analysis.

The Affective Computing group, directed by Jonathan Gratch with research leads Gale Lucas and Mohammad Soleymani, studies the role of emotion in human-machine and interpersonal interaction: automatic analysis of multimodal emotional signals, computational models of how emotions influence social cognition, and applications to human-machine teaming and interpersonal skills training.

The Learning Sciences group, led by Benjamin Nye, investigates how AI can increase learning and help learners accomplish their goals. Its work spans adaptive learning for upskilling (the PAL3 framework), virtual mentoring at scale (the MentorPal platform), intelligent tutoring (OpenTutor), engagement data mining, and social learning in augmented reality. It operates the AI Research Center of Excellence for Education (AIRCOEE) and the USC Center for Generative AI and Society.

The Medical Virtual Reality group (MedVR), directed by Albert "Skip" Rizzo, researches the design, development, and evaluation of virtual and augmented reality systems for clinical assessment, treatment, and rehabilitation, spanning psychological, cognitive, and motor functioning. Notable projects include BRAVEMIND, which uses VR exposure therapy to help trauma survivors reprocess difficult memories related to PTSD. See https://ict.usc.edu/research/labs-groups/medical-virtual-reality/ for details.

The Mixed Reality Lab (MxR), directed by David Nelson, explores emerging human-machine interactions and develops hardware and system solutions. Established in 2008, its VR and AR research influenced high-end headset development such as the Oculus Rift and inspired affordable options like Google Cardboard. Current focus areas include extended reality user interfaces and experiences, spatial data visualization, adaptive human-machine interfaces, generative AI content creation, and immersive story-based learning.

The Dialogue Group, led by David Traum, computationally analyzes human dialogue and develops computational models for dialogue systems: spoken and natural language understanding, dialogue management, natural language generation, and speech synthesis. A key focus is developing automated human-like role-play characters for simulation and training of communication-related skills.

The Narrative Group, led by Andrew Gordon, pursues research in automated storytelling and narrative understanding, combining logical abduction with large-scale commonsense knowledge, generative AI models for content creation, and hybrid neural, probabilistic, and symbolic reasoning. Applications include automated movie interpretation, rapid generation of simulated training environments, and creative filmmaking tools.

The Vision and Graphics Lab, directed by Yajie Zhao, creates realistic virtual characters through advanced digitization, built on the Academy Award-winning Light Stage capture technology developed at ICT. The Light Stage has been used in dozens of films, and the lab develops AI-integrated workflows for avatar creation, editing, and rendering, with future work extending to scene and terrain understanding.

The Human-Centered AI Lab, led by Ning Wang, researches human-AI collaborative problem-solving, AI ethics and society, AI education and AI for education, trust between humans and AI, and persuasive AI. The lab develops educational games and interactive exhibits that make AI concepts accessible to diverse audiences.

The Social Simulation Lab, with interim research lead Nik Gurney, models and simulates human social interaction within AI systems, using decision-theoretic AI as the foundation for a reusable theory of mind. Its open-source platform PsychSim has been applied to large-scale urban population simulations and small-scale human-machine coordination, and powers interactive training games for negotiation, language and culture learning, and risk behavior prevention.

The Human-inspired Adaptive Teaming Systems (HATS) Lab, led by Volkan Ustun, develops autonomous synthetic characters and NPCs for military training simulations, combining multi-agent reinforcement learning with graph neural networks and drawing on operations research, cognitive science, and game theory. The group previously built the Sigma cognitive architecture and now also develops interactive decision support systems for simulation environments.

The Geospatial Terrain Research group, led by Andrew Feng, develops high-resolution 3D geospatial databases for military simulations and virtual environments: UAV data capture, photogrammetric processing, and semantic segmentation producing simulation-ready terrain. The team has created 3D reconstructions for over a hundred sites and contributes to the Army's One World Server, with current research in neural 3D terrain reconstruction and zero-shot semantic segmentation.

The Technology Evaluation Lab, led by Gale Lucas, conducts research in human-computer, human-robot, and human-building interaction, studying how people build relationships, rapport, and trust with machines, and how technology can persuade, negotiate, or influence human behavior. The lab is known for rigorous user-study design and best practices in evaluation methodology.

The Virtual Human Therapeutics Lab (VHTL), directed by Sharon Mozgai, uses embodied conversational AI agents within evidence-based mobile health applications to explore how virtual humans can advance mental, emotional, and physical health and behavior change, with an emphasis on equitable and accountable AI practices. See https://ict.usc.edu/research/labs-groups/virtual-human-therapeutics-lab/ for details.

The Simulations, Architectures & Intelligent Agents (SAIA) Lab, led by Arno Hartholt, develops and integrates virtual humans and simulation technology, including the RIDE platform and the Virtual Human Toolkit (VHToolkit). See the SAIA Lab overview document for details: https://ict.usc.edu/research/labs-groups/saia/
